### 1. What the Authors Did (Dataset Methodology)

When you mention "four datasets", you are likely referring to the four distinct data distributions present in your workspace: **DisasterM3 (Bench & Instruct)** and **RSVLM-QA (plus its 4 internal source datasets)**. Here is exactly what the authors did for each:

#### **Dataset 1 & 2: DisasterM3 (Bench & Instruct)**
The authors of DisasterM3 focused on evaluating how well Vision-Language Models understand disaster scenarios (earthquakes, floods, damaged infrastructure) from remote sensing imagery. 
* **What they did:** They collected thousands of drone and satellite images of post-disaster scenarios. They then built two distinct subsets:
  * **DisasterM3_Bench (~17K samples):** A strict evaluation benchmark containing multiple-choice and free-text tasks (e.g., "count the damaged buildings", "identify the disaster bearing bodies").
  * **DisasterM3_Instruct (~162K samples):** A massively scaled version used purely for fine-tuning models.
* **Their Evaluation:** They scored **14 different VLMs** (ranging from commercial closed-source like GPT-4o to open-source models). They discovered that smaller models failed completely unless given highly rigid prompt templates (e.g., "Your task is to respond with ONLY the capital letter"). They finally fine-tuned **InternVL3** and **Qwen2.5-VL** on the dataset to create highly competitive baselines.

#### **Dataset 3 & 4: RSVLM-QA (and its 4 Sub-Datasets)**
The authors of RSVLM-QA aimed to build the most content-rich, massive Visual Question Answering (VQA) dataset for general Remote Sensing imagery.
* **What they did:** Unlike DisasterM3 which focused on disasters, they took four major pre-existing segmentation datasets (**WHU, LoveDA, INRIA, iSAID**) and integrated them. 
* **Their Innovation:** They built a "dual-track" automated pipeline. First, they fed segmentation data to scripts to mathematically calculate the exact count of buildings and roads. Then, they used GPT-4 Vision (GPT-4.1) to generate incredibly detailed spatial relations, tags, and complex image captions. 
* **Their Evaluation:** They benchmarked **6 modern VLMs**, using GPT-4.1 as the "expert judge" to evaluate free-text VQA. They found that **Ovis2** (70.2%) and **InternVL3** (61.7%) performed the best, while older models like BLIP-2 severely struggled.
